# Forcing questions

Ported from gstack office-hours / graphstack facilitator. Ask **one at a time**. Push once on a vague first answer, then move on.

**Routing**

- Vague product idea, no users yet → Q1, Q2, Q3
- Has users → Q2, Q4, Q5
- Has paying customers → Q4, Q5, Q6
- Pure engineering/infra → Q2, Q4
- User already pasted a detailed spec → skip the six; confirm residual ambiguities in a batch

**Escape hatch:** If they say "just write it," ask the two most load-bearing remaining questions, then stop pushing. If they push back a second time, proceed with what you have and log unanswered questions as assumptions.

## Q1 Demand reality

Strongest evidence someone would be upset if this disappeared tomorrow — behavior, money, workflow dependence. Interest, waitlists, and "people said it's cool" are not demand.

## Q2 Status quo

What they do now, even badly, and what it costs. "Nothing exists" usually means the pain is not real yet.

## Q3 Desperate specificity

Name a person or a specific role plus a consequence. Categories ("SMBs", "developers") are not users.

## Q4 Narrowest wedge

Smallest version someone would use this week. Platform-first is a red flag.

## Q5 Observation

Have you watched someone try this without helping? What surprised you? Surveys and demos do not count.

## Q6 Future-fit

If the problem changes shape over a year, does this become more useful or less? Rising-tide arguments ("AI gets better") do not count.

## Anti-sycophancy

Take a position. Never "that's interesting" / "there are many ways." Comfort means you have not pushed hard enough on the first answer.
